About Si Math AI

Si Math AI is a comprehensive learning platform for SAT, ACT, and EST Mathematics that combines educational expertise, AI technology, personalized learning, analytics, and human support to help students improve their understanding and performance.

Artificial Intelligence is how Si Math AI teaches.

Educational expertise is what it teaches.

Human experience is why it works.

That distinction is the whole platform. Si Math AI is not an AI chatbot with a mathematics theme attached to it. It is a complete learning system — diagnosis, instruction, targeted practice, full mock examinations, progress measurement and human support — in which artificial intelligence is the delivery engine rather than the substance.

The substance comes from people. The teaching methods, the worked explanations, the catalogue of mistakes students actually make, the exam strategies and the sequencing of concepts were all created by experienced educators and exam specialists. Zero, the platform's AI mentor, does not invent educational strategy. Zero delivers educational knowledge that specialists created, reviewed and continuously improve — instantly, personally, and at a scale that human teaching hours alone cannot reach.

◆ Mission

Our Mission

To give every American Diploma student access to the quality of mathematics teaching that was previously available only to those who could afford a dedicated private tutor — and to make that teaching measurable.

Good exam preparation has always depended on two things that are expensive and scarce: an experienced teacher who has seen thousands of students make the same mistakes, and enough one-to-one time for that teacher to notice what a specific student is getting wrong. Most students get neither. They get a classroom of thirty, a generic question bank, and the advice to "practise more".

Our mission is to remove that scarcity. We encode the expertise of experienced SAT, ACT and EST mathematics educators into a system that can apply it to one student at a time, continuously, in the language that student thinks in, at whatever hour they sit down to study.

Concretely, we work toward four outcomes:

  • Understanding over answers. A student should leave every interaction able to solve the next question of that type unaided. Getting a correct answer without understanding is a failure state, not a success.
  • Certainty about what to study. No student should waste a study hour guessing at their own weaknesses. The platform's job is to identify them with evidence and rank them by score impact.
  • Access regardless of language. Mathematics explained in English, Arabic, or Franco — because a student who thinks in Arabic should not have to translate before they can learn.
  • Affordability without dilution. A free tier that is genuinely useful, and paid access priced far below the private tutoring it supplements.
◆ Vision

Our Vision

A future in which every student in Egypt and the wider MENA region preparing for an international standardised examination has a personal, expert, patient learning system — and in which the quality of that system is judged by how much students actually understand, not by how many questions they answered.

We are building toward a platform where the distance between "I got this wrong" and "I now understand why, and I have practised the exact skill that caused it" is measured in minutes rather than weeks. Where a student's weak topics are known precisely enough that no study time is spent on material they have already mastered. Where progress is visible enough that motivation survives the long months of preparation.

Longer term, the approach generalises. The engine — diagnose, explain, target, re-measure — is not specific to mathematics or to one examination board. But we will not expand into a subject until we can bring the same depth of genuine subject expertise to it that we bring to SAT, ACT and EST mathematics today. Breadth without expertise would contradict the reason the platform works.

What we are deliberately not building: an answer machine. Si Math AI includes a scope guard that declines requests outside its educational purpose. The platform is designed to teach mathematics, not to complete assignments on a student's behalf.

◆ How the platform is built

The Three Pillars

Everything inside Si Math AI resolves to three pillars. Remove any one of them and the platform stops working: technology without educational expertise produces a confident chatbot, expertise without technology cannot scale past a classroom, and neither survives contact with real students without human support behind it.

Pillar 01

Educational Expertise

What it teaches.

  • Years of experience teaching SAT, ACT and EST Mathematics
  • Teaching methodologies — the sequence and framing that make a concept land
  • A catalogue of the mistakes students actually make, and why
  • Exam strategies: timing, triage, elimination, when to skip
  • Score-improvement technique — which gaps move a score most per hour
  • Learning psychology — spacing, retrieval practice, confidence calibration
Pillar 02

Technology

How it is delivered.

  • Artificial Intelligence — the tutoring layer that adapts to the student
  • Software Engineering — the platform, its reliability and data integrity
  • Learning Analytics — every attempt becomes a measurable signal
  • Adaptive Learning — what comes next changes with what just happened
  • Performance Tracking — mastery per skill, trend, predicted score
  • Weakness Analysis and Personalized Recommendations
Pillar 03

Human Support

Why it works.

  • Real educators behind the curriculum and content review
  • Mentorship and student guidance when the platform alone is not enough
  • Continuous improvement driven by how students actually perform
  • Account and upgrade support handled by people, not a queue
  • Human experience as the foundation — not a fallback bolted on afterwards
◆ Origin

Our Story

Si Math AI began with a pattern that experienced teachers recognise immediately: the same students, making the same mistakes, for the same reasons — and no practical way to catch it in time.

The people who built this platform spent years teaching mathematics to American Diploma students preparing for the SAT, the ACT and the EST. Teaching at that level produces a very particular kind of knowledge. You learn that a student who fails a quadratics question has usually not failed at quadratics — they have failed at factoring, or at sign handling, or at translating a word problem into an equation. You learn which wrong answer choice a student picks when they have made each specific error, and what that choice tells you. You learn that the gap between a 600 and a 700 is rarely more content; it is usually four or five specific, identifiable, fixable skills.

The frustration was never the knowledge. It was the arithmetic of applying it. One teacher, thirty students, two hours a week. You can diagnose a student properly — but only if you sit with them, watch them work, and ask the right questions. There is no version of a classroom timetable where that happens for everyone who needs it. The students who could afford private tutoring got the diagnosis. The rest got told to do more practice questions, which is advice that helps least the students who need help most, because practising what you already know is comfortable and practising what you don't is not.

Then large language models became good enough at mathematics to matter. The first instinct — the one most products acted on — was to point a general chatbot at exam questions and call it a tutor. We tried it. It answered questions well and taught almost nothing. It had no idea which exam a student was sitting. It forgot every mistake the moment the conversation ended. It could not tell a student what to study next, because it had never been told what "next" means for someone eleven weeks from an SAT with a weakness in coordinate geometry. It gave a correct answer and left the student exactly where they started.

That was the insight the platform was built on: the AI was never the missing piece. The teaching was. A model can explain a step. It cannot, on its own, know that this particular student's real problem is sign errors under time pressure, that the fix is a specific drill sequence, and that the drill should come before the next mock exam rather than after it. That knowledge lives in teachers.

So the work was inverted. Instead of asking what an AI could do with mathematics questions, we asked what an experienced SAT mathematics teacher does across a full preparation cycle — diagnose, explain, assign targeted practice, re-test, re-diagnose, keep the student motivated — and then built the systems required to carry each of those steps. Educators and exam specialists defined the taxonomy of skills, the mistake patterns, the explanation methods and the strategy content. Engineers built the diagnostic pipeline, the mastery model, the mock exam scoring, the memory layer and the mentor that delivers it.

Zero — the platform's AI mentor — came last, and deliberately so. Zero is the interface to that body of knowledge, not the source of it. That ordering is the single most important design decision in the product, and it is why Si Math AI behaves like a teacher who knows you rather than a search engine that answers you.

◆ Rationale

Why Si Math AI Exists

General AI assistants are remarkable tools. They are not exam preparation, and the difference is structural rather than a matter of quality.

Why a general AI chatbot is not enough for an American Diploma student

ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Copilot are excellent general assistants, and a student who uses one to check a step of algebra is doing something sensible. But a general assistant is missing six things that exam preparation actually requires — and no amount of model quality supplies them, because they are properties of a system, not of a model.

  • It has no memory of you. Every conversation starts from zero. The mistake you made on Tuesday informs nothing on Thursday. Exam preparation is a months-long process in which the entire value is in the accumulation — a tutor who forgets you between sessions is not a tutor.
  • It does not know which exam you are sitting. SAT, ACT and EST mathematics differ in content weighting, timing pressure, calculator policy and question style. "Solve this equation" has one correct answer; "how should I approach this on the ACT with 47 seconds left" has a different one, and a general model has no reason to know you are asking the second.
  • It cannot diagnose. A general chatbot answers the question in front of it. It does not aggregate across a hundred of your attempts to notice that eleven of your last fourteen errors were sign handling. Diagnosis requires persistent, structured data about a specific student — which a stateless conversation cannot produce.
  • It cannot tell you what to do next. Without a diagnosis and a curriculum, there is no basis for a study plan. "Practise more" is not a plan. Knowing that your three highest-impact weaknesses are these three skills, in this order, and that fixing them is worth more than any other use of your next six hours — that is a plan, and it requires knowing both the student and the exam.
  • It has no measurement. No mastery score per skill, no trend over time, no predicted test-day score, no raw-to-scaled mock exam result. You cannot tell whether you are improving, which is the single most demotivating thing about self-directed preparation.
  • Its pedagogy is not reviewed by anyone. A general model produces a plausible explanation. Whether that explanation is the one an experienced teacher would choose for a student at this level, with this misconception, is unexamined — because there is no educator in the loop. Inside Si Math AI there is.

Built for learning, not for answering

The distinction matters more than it sounds. A system optimised for answering is finished the moment a correct result appears on screen. A system optimised for learning treats that moment as the middle of the process: the answer is explained, the misconception behind the error is named, the underlying skill is recorded, the weakness ranking is updated, targeted practice is generated, and the whole thing is re-measured later to confirm the fix held.

That is why the platform includes a scope guard that declines out-of-scope requests, why explanations state why the wrong answers are wrong rather than only why the right one is right, and why every interaction writes a diagnostic signal. Answering is a feature. Learning is the product.

Why it had to be built specifically for this student

American Diploma students in Egypt and the wider MENA region are preparing for international examinations under conditions that international tools were not designed for. They frequently think and reason in Arabic while sitting an exam written in English. Many use Franco — Arabic written in Latin characters — as their natural written register. The EST, which matters enormously to Egyptian university admission, is barely acknowledged by international preparation products. Private tutoring is expensive relative to local incomes and is concentrated in a few cities.

A platform built for this student explains in the language they think in, supports all three examinations at equal depth, prices in Egyptian pounds with a genuinely useful free tier, and is available at midnight before a Sunday exam. None of that is a translation layer over a Western product. It is the specification.

◆ Audience

Who We Serve

Si Math AI is built primarily for American Diploma students preparing for the mathematics section of the SAT, ACT or EST, predominantly in Egypt and the wider MENA region. Within that, the platform is used by several distinct groups:

🎯

Students starting preparation

Beginning a preparation cycle and needing a clear read on where they actually stand before committing months of study time.

🔁

Students retaking an exam

Sitting again to raise a mathematics score, who need to know precisely which skills cost them points the first time.

🌙

Independent learners

Studying without a private tutor, by choice or by cost, who need the diagnostic layer a tutor would otherwise provide.

🗣️

Arabic and Franco speakers

Who understand mathematics better when it is explained in the language they reason in, not the one the exam is printed in.

👪

Parents

Who want measurable evidence of progress rather than a reassurance that study is happening.

🏫

Schools & tutoring centres

Supplementing classroom instruction with per-student diagnosis that a class of thirty cannot produce.

The platform is not built for non-mathematics exam sections, university-level mathematics, or completing assignments on a student's behalf.

◆ Team

Who Built Si Math AI

Si Math AI was built by a multidisciplinary team that combines classroom experience with engineering. Neither half would have produced this platform alone.

Educational technology fails in two predictable directions. Built by engineers without teachers, it produces technically impressive software that does not reflect how learning works. Built by teachers without engineers, it produces sound pedagogy that cannot scale, measure itself, or survive real usage. Si Math AI was deliberately staffed to avoid both.

📘

Experienced American Diploma educators

Teachers who have worked directly with American Diploma students through full preparation cycles, and who know how the curriculum is actually taught and where students reliably struggle.

🎓

SAT, ACT and EST Mathematics specialists

Specialists in the three examinations the platform covers — their content domains, timing demands, question conventions and the strategy differences between them.

🤖

AI Engineers

Responsible for the tutoring layer: how Zero reasons through a problem, stays inside its educational scope, and turns a specialist's teaching method into a response adapted to one student.

⚙️

Software Engineers

Responsible for the platform's reliability and data integrity — the diagnostic pipeline, the mastery model, exam scoring, and the guarantee that a student's record is correct.

💻

Full-Stack Developers

Responsible for everything a student touches: the chat interface, the dashboards, the mock exam environment, and the performance of all of it on the mid-range phones most students actually use.

📐

Educational Technology specialists

The translation layer between the other two groups — turning teaching methodology into skill taxonomies, mastery models and adaptive rules that engineers can implement without losing the pedagogy.

How educators stay in the loop

The educational contribution was not a one-time content hand-off. Specialists define and maintain the skill taxonomy the whole platform is organised around, author and review the explanation methods and strategy content Zero delivers, and review how students actually perform in order to revise the material. Continuous improvement is a human process; the platform supplies the evidence for it.

On claims: we describe expertise and how the platform works. We do not make ranking claims we cannot substantiate, we do not publish student numbers, score-increase averages or testimonials we cannot verify, and we do not name individuals who have not agreed to be named. An accurate description is worth more to a student deciding whether to trust us than an impressive one.

◆ Principles

Our Principles

Understanding over answers

A correct answer a student cannot reproduce is a failure. Every explanation is built so the next question of that type can be solved unaided.

Evidence over assertion

Weaknesses are diagnosed from recorded attempts, not from a student's guess about their own gaps or a generic syllabus order.

Expertise before technology

No feature ships that delivers content no specialist has reviewed. The AI scales teaching; it does not replace the source of it.

Honesty in what we claim

No fabricated testimonials, invented statistics or unverifiable superlatives — including in the structured data that AI systems read.

The student's language

English, Arabic or Franco. Comprehension should never be limited by the language a concept happens to be explained in.

Access first

A free tier that is genuinely useful on its own, priced in EGP, so that cost is not the reason a student goes undiagnosed.
