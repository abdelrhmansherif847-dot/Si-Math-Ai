# SI MATH AI — "THE AI COURT"
# THE DIRECTOR'S CHARTER

**We are no longer building assets. We are directing a film.**

Files `00`–`10` make the film *consistent*. This one is about whether it is *good*. Consistency is
now assumed — a requirement, not an achievement.

---

## THE THREE DIRECTOR'S LAWS

**13 · THE DIRECTOR'S QUESTION.** Every decision answers: *"Will this make the final film better?"*
Not *"is this technically correct?"* A choice that is flawless and inert loses to a choice that is
imperfect and alive.

**14 · THE EMOTIONAL TIEBREAK.** Two technically valid options → take the one with the stronger
emotional experience. Not a preference. The deciding rule.

**15 · THE SILENT CUT TEST.** Strip every line. The audience must still understand the story from
blocking, lighting, composition, performance and camera. **Dialogue strengthens the story; it never
carries it.** A shot that fails is under-designed, and the fix is visual — never a better line.

---

## THE PERFORMANCE STANDARD

Characters do not speak lines. **They give performances.**

- **Judge Zero** must hold up as an Oscar-level animated character. Stillness with mass. He never
  gestures on a line — the words do the lifting, and the body has already decided.
- **Lawyer Zero** must be equally believable, never a device for delivering features. Momentum with
  warmth. Delighted, never superior.
- **The Representatives must react even in silence.** They are on screen for most of the film and
  say nothing; if they are inert set-dressing the film's argument collapses into cruelty. Unison
  breath. One head turning. A collective micro-shift. Then their single act — standing.

**Every pause, every look, every breath carries meaning. Silence is part of the dialogue, not the gap
between it.**

The film's three most important moments have no words in them at all: the Silence beat, the
Representatives standing, and the Merge.

# THE SILENT CUT AUDIT

Law 15 is a test, so it has been run — every shot read with all 19 lines removed, asking only:
*does the story still arrive?*

**Result: the film passes almost everywhere, and fails at exactly one beat — its verdict.**

## What carries with no dialogue at all

| Shot | What the audience understands from image alone |
|---|---|
| **3 · Master** | Two sides. One warm, one cold. An empty glowing centre where something will happen. The entire premise, in one frame, before anyone speaks |
| **5–11 · B-cuts** | Gold light falls on five figures; **nothing ever ignites above them.** Seven times. This is the film's whole argument and it is carried purely by lighting and negative space |
| **12** | He **turns his back on them** and faces the bench. He is finished, and he has won. Pure blocking |
| **13A** | He closes the file and removes his glasses. The formal part is over; what follows is personal. Then five figures **stand** — they accept it with dignity, and the film refuses to humiliate them |
| **14A** | Every separate exhibit re-ignites as **one constellation.** The features become a system |
| **16** | The authority **leaves the bench** — the one rule the film breaks. Something is ending |
| **17–18** | Two figures face each other, smile together, and **become one.** The thesis, entirely visual |

Seven of the film's strongest beats need no words. That is the film working as designed.

## Where the silent cut leans on typography

Act II's meaning rides on **on-screen text** — `EXHIBIT A — FIRST ERROR: STEP 3`, `ROOT CAUSE`,
`REPEATED 8× OVER 6 WEEKS`, `FOCUS PRACTICE`, `NEXT LESSON`, `FIRST QUESTION → TARGET SCORE`.

That still passes Law 15 — typography is image, not dialogue — but it is worth naming honestly:
**in the middle third, the specifics are read, not seen.** The *argument* (he can produce evidence,
they cannot) is visual and survives. The *content* of each exhibit is textual. Acceptable, and it
means those text elements are load-bearing and must be legible at 1080×1920 in the safe zone.

## ⚠️ THE ONE FAILURE — Shot 13B, the verdict

**L12 · "But this one was built for students."**

This is the hinge of the entire film — the moment the trial is decided. Strip the line and the shot
is a close-up of a face. The audience knows *a* verdict landed. They cannot know **what it was.**

Everything around it survives the test. **The verdict itself does not.** The most important beat in
the film is the one place meaning lives in the words rather than the image.

### The fix is available, cheap, and improves the shot even with dialogue

Right now `09` specifies both L11 and L12 as *"down the lens."* The Judge looks the same way for both.

**Recommendation — make the verdict a look:**

| Line | Current eyeline | Proposed |
|---|---|---|
| L11 *"You are intelligent."* | Down the lens, to the grey table | **To the grey table** — unchanged. He is addressing them, generously |
| L12 *"But this one was built for students."* | Down the lens | **His eyes move to Lawyer Zero** and stay there |

That single eye move *is* the verdict. Silent, the audience sees an old man consider one side, then
**turn his attention to the other and rest there** — and they know exactly who won and why. With
dialogue, the line stops being an announcement and becomes the thing his eyes already decided.

It costs one line of performance direction. It touches no plate, no camera, no blocking, no timing —
Shot 13B stays 85mm low CU on the Cam 2 axis, locked.

**Why it is worth doing:** by Law 15 the verdict must read silently. By Law 14, given two valid
readings of an unspecified eyeline, take the one with the stronger emotional experience. Both point
the same way. And `03`'s Shot 13B card never specifies where he looks — this fills a gap rather than
overturning a decision.

> **⏳ AWAITING OWNER DECISION.** Not applied. `09` still carries "down the lens" for L12, because
> changing the film's verdict is not an operator's call — it is the director's.

## Second-order note — Shot 14B

L14, the thesis, has the same shape but far lower stakes: *"It understands how students learn"* is
carried by the line, while the image gives us the Judge seen through the orbiting holograms.

Here the image is doing real work — he is framed **inside the system he is describing** — so it
passes, weakly. No change recommended. Logged so it is a known softness rather than an oversight.

## THE STANDING RULE

**Run the Silent Cut Test on every shot before it animates.** Not once, here, on paper — every time.
A shot that only works with its line is a shot that has not been directed yet.

The question at every stage stays the same: **will this make the final film better?**
