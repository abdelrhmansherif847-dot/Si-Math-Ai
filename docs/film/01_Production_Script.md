**Format:** 9:16 vertical (1080×1920) · **Hero cut:** 1:16 · **Extended cut:** 2:56
**Medium:** Fully animated 3D CG short. Not live action. Not motion graphics with a mascot pasted on.

## v5 — THE ZERO LAW *(read this before anything else)*

> **There is one Zero, at two stages of life.** Judge Zero is the elder. Lawyer Zero is the young Zero. Both are produced by **costume swap from their own master art** — never invented, never reinterpreted.

### The two masters

| | **JUDGE ZERO** | **LAWYER ZERO** |
|---|---|---|
| **Master art** | The elder Zero illustration — white beard, round gold glasses, cane | The young Zero illustration — the one with the red cape |
| **Costume swap** | Brown cloak → black-and-gold judicial robe | Red cape → tailored charcoal suit, white shirt, deep red tie, gold lapel pin |
| **Keeps** | Beard, glasses, cane | No beard, no glasses, no cane |
| **Reads as** | **Wisdom & Experience** | **Energy & Action** |
| **Bearing** | Settled, weighted, still. 0.7× baseline speed | Upright, springy, forward. 1.2× baseline speed |

**The red and gold carry over.** The cape's red becomes the tie; the cape's gold medallion becomes the lapel pin. That is how the audience's eye connects suited Zero back to the mascot they already know.

### Frozen in both — never regenerated, never reinterpreted

face shape · head-to-body ratio · eyes and their highlight shapes · snout · horn-fins · wings and pink membrane · body proportions · tail and spikes · belly plates · cyan colour palette · **art style** · line weight · shading technique.

**The only differences permitted between them:** age markers that already exist in the two master illustrations (beard, glasses), the outfit, and the performance.

### The test
Strip the costume off either version. What remains must be pixel-identical to that version's master art. If the face, proportions, or rendering style shifted at all, it's wrong — regardless of how good the image looks.

### Why this kept going wrong *(documented so it doesn't recur)*
Words like *"3D render," "Pixar-quality," "subsurface scattering," "feature-animation production quality," "realistic," "sculpted"* all read to an image model as **permission to redesign**. They are the failure mode. Every time one appeared in a prompt, Zero's identity was lost.

**The working prompt structure — use it for every Zero asset, forever:**
1. `Costume swap only. Keep the attached character and its art style completely unchanged.`
2. Enumerate every frozen feature explicitly.
3. `Do NOT redraw / restyle / make realistic / change proportions.`
4. `THE ONLY CHANGE: [the outfit].`
5. Attach the **master art** — never a previous generation.

**Status: PERMANENT.** This is the standing method for every future Zero variation, on this film and everything after it.

## WHAT CHANGED IN v2 — AND WHY## WHAT CHANGED IN v2 — AND WHY

The first draft had Judge Zero asking all 26 questions himself. With two characters, that structure is now wrong — a judge who argues the case is not a judge, and the "trial" reads as a monologue.

**The new engine:**

> **Lawyer Zero prosecutes. Judge Zero rules.**

Lawyer Zero cross-examines — he asks the questions, and each answer materialises as a courtroom **exhibit**. He is fast, sharp, charismatic, and he *moves*. Judge Zero says almost nothing for 40 seconds, and then delivers the verdict — and because he has been silent, the verdict lands like a hammer.

This gives us three things the single-character version could not have:
1. **Contrast in motion.** Lawyer Zero at 1.2× speed against Judge Zero at 0.7× speed. The camera has something to cut between.
2. **A reason for the audience to wait.** We're not watching a feature list. We're waiting for a ruling.
3. **The final image writes itself.** Both Zeros standing side by side in the dark — the same character, young and old. Growth, embodied. That *is* the product.

## ACT IV — THE MERGE (0:57 – 1:12)

> This is the sequence the whole film exists to deliver. It runs almost entirely without dialogue. **Do not cut it for time — cut Act II instead.** Total runtime moves to 1:12; the extra seven seconds buy the ending.

### SHOT 16 · 0:57 – 1:02 — *He Steps Down*
**Visual:** The gallery, the grey bench, the exhibits — everything dissolves into blackness from the edges inward until only the two of them remain, each in his own shaft of light, the polished marble still reflecting beneath them.

Judge Zero rises. He sets down the gavel. And for the first time in the film, **he steps down from the bench** — slowly, deliberately, one stair at a time, the robe trailing behind him. The camera has never seen him leave that bench. The audience has been waiting an entire film for this without knowing it.

**Camera:** Slow crane down, matching his descent, ending at floor level.
**Animation:** The cane takes real weight on each step. The robe has genuine cloth simulation. His descent stays at 0.7× — he does not hurry, even now.
**[JZ]:** "Students don't need another chatbot."
**SFX:** Cane on marble. Cloth. A single low sustained string note entering underneath.

### SHOT 17 · 1:02 – 1:07 — *Face to Face*
**Visual:** Lawyer Zero steps out from the defence table and walks toward him. Two shafts of light converging. They stop face to face, close — near enough that the two identical faces are unmistakably the same face.

A beat of stillness.

**Then both of them smile.** Not a grin — a warm, knowing, unhurried smile, the kind you give someone you have known your whole life. Because they have.

**Camera:** Slow dolly around them, 90°, ending in profile two-shot so both faces read equally.
**Animation:** This is the single most important piece of facial animation in the film. Land the smile and the film works. Miss it and nothing else matters. Give the smile its own animation pass and its own review.
**[LZ]** *(same voice, lighter, closer):* "They need a mentor."
**SFX:** Music swells. Everything else falls away.

### SHOT 18 · 1:07 – 1:12 — *One Zero*
**Visual:** Between them, soft **blue energy** begins to rise from the marble floor — the same cyan as their scales, luminous, weightless. It curls upward around both figures in slow ribbons, particles of blue light drifting up through the volumetric beams.

The light intensifies. The robe and the suit dissolve into streams of blue energy that spiral inward. The two silhouettes move toward each other and **become one.**

The light collapses to a single soft pulse — and **one Zero** stands alone in the shaft of light. No robe. No suit. Just Zero, exactly as he has always been, looking directly down the lens.

He smiles once. Blackout.

**Camera:** Slow push-in through the merge, holding as the light resolves, then a beat on the single figure before black.
**Animation:** The merge is *warm and gentle*, never a violent VFX transformation. Think two candle flames becoming one — not a teleporter. Blue energy only; no white flash, no lens flare, no glitch effects.
**Colour:** `#29B6F0` core, `#7FDCFF` in the highlights, drifting particles at 40% opacity.
**VO:** — *(none. The image carries it. Adding a line here would break the film.)*
**SFX:** The music resolves onto a single held chord. A soft harmonic swell on the merge. Then silence.

**What this shot says, without saying it:** the one who examines and the one who mentors are the same system. That is the entire product thesis, delivered as an image instead of a claim.

## VOICE CASTING — **one actor, one voice, two energies**

Because this is one character, it is **one voice actor recording both roles.** Do not cast two people.

| | **In the robe** | **In the suit** |
|---|---|---|
| **Timbre** | Identical | Identical |
| **Tempo** | Slow. Full stops. ~2.5s per sentence | Fast, rhythmic, momentum-driven |
| **Placement** | Low, chest, settled | Forward, brighter, crisper consonants |
| **Energy** | Weighted, conclusive | Alive, probing, enjoying himself |
| **Booth direction** | "You already know how this ends. You're speaking so the room hears it." | "You're not asking questions. You're setting a trap, and you're enjoying it." |
| **Never** | Loud. Angry. Selling. | Smug. Sarcastic. Mocking the other side. |

**⚠️ REVISIT — Lawyer Zero is young again.** You chose *"same voice, different energy"* when both Zeros were the same age. Now that Lawyer Zero is the young Zero, a single 55–70 voice on both no longer fits the picture. Three options, in order of recommendation:

1. **One actor with real range** — the same timbre played young (brighter, forward, faster) and old (low, weighted, slow). Best of both: audibly one character, visibly two ages. Casting is harder; worth it.
2. **Two actors, matched timbre** — cast the young voice *to* the elder's placement so they sound related. Safer to cast, slightly less magical.
3. **Same voice, no differentiation** — what you originally picked. Now risks sounding like the young dragon is being dubbed by his grandfather.

**Judge Zero:** 55–70, deep chest resonance, no vocal fry. Morgan Freeman's tempo × a tenured professor's precision.
**Lawyer Zero:** 25–32, bright, forward placement, crisp consonants, momentum.

**Record both roles in one session regardless** — alternating lines, same room, same mic. That's what makes them sound like they belong to each other.

## THE VISUAL WORLD — *the Si Math AI Court*

**Style target: Arcane × Spider-Verse × premium anime cinematic.** Not a real courthouse. Not architectural realism. A place that could only exist inside the Si Math AI universe.

### The style rules

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Painterly, hand-painted surface texture — visible brushwork | Photoreal marble, PBR scans, physically accurate materials |
| Bold graphic shapes, strong silhouettes, exaggerated proportions | Correct architectural proportion or real-world scale |
| Hard cel-shaded light separation — light and shadow as *shapes* | Soft realistic global illumination falloff |
| Rich saturated colour, extreme contrast, crushed blacks | Grey, neutral, "tasteful" desaturation |
| Impossible geometry: floating platforms, columns with no top | Anything that reads as a building you could actually construct |
| Chromatic edges, halation, graphic light streaks | Realistic lens simulation |

**The test:** pause on any frame. Could it be a still from a live-action film? Then it's wrong. It should be unmistakably *painted*.

### Architecture
- **Columns** impossibly tall and tapering, dissolving into indigo darkness with no visible top.
- **Floating platforms** and suspended stone galleries, held up by nothing.
- **Deep navy and midnight blue** stone with glowing warm gold inlay tracing every edge — the gold reads as circuitry that has been carved rather than printed.
- **The ceiling does not exist.** It becomes sky, then void.
- **Scale is emotional, not literal.** Zero is small. The system around him is vast.

### Light
- **Hard volumetric god rays** cut down through haze in defined visible shafts — graphic beams, not soft glow.
- **Cyan-blue magic light** as the second key: holograms, particles, and the merge all emit it.
- **Warm gold** as the accent against deep navy — the only warm colour in the world.
- **Light as shape.** Cel-separated pools with defined edges. Shadow is a graphic element, not an absence.

### Holograms & Floating UI
Every capability appears as a **magical blue hologram**, never a screenshot:
- Translucent cyan panels with glowing edges, slight chromatic fringing
- Mathematical symbols, equations and graphs drifting as ambient particles
- Panels ignite with a burst and settle with a soft ripple
- They cast real light onto the marble, the columns, and Zero's face
- Streams of blue light particles rise continuously through the room

## THE COURTROOM IS A CHARACTER — *ambient animation spec*

The room is cast, not built. It is present in every scene, it reacts, and it is never still. **No frame in this film contains a motionless environment.**

### Always running — in every single shot, background or foreground

| Element | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| **Blue particles** | Continuous slow upward drift through the whole volume. Density higher in the light shafts. Never stops, not even in the locked-off verdict |
| **Energy veins** | Cyan light flows *through* the stone architecture like slow-moving veins — always in motion, direction consistent shot to shot |
| **God rays** | Shafts shift and breathe slowly as if something enormous is moving above the ceiling. Dust and haze roll through them |
| **Floating holograms** | Ambient panels, equations and glowing math symbols drift and slowly rotate in the mid-ground at all times |
| **Debris** | Small stone fragments and glyphs hang weightless, rotating almost imperceptibly |
| **Floor reflections** | Every light source doubled, with a slow shimmer moving across the polished surface |

### The Seal breathes

The **Si emblem** behind the bench has its own performance — treat it as a third character:

- **At rest:** a slow luminous pulse, roughly one cycle every 4 seconds. Like breathing.
- **On each exhibit:** a brighter pulse on the ignition frame, and the energy veins in the architecture surge outward from it. The room *acknowledges the evidence.*
- **On the verdict:** it holds at full brightness through Judge Zero's ruling, then dims as the room dissolves.
- **On the merge:** it is the last thing to fade — the light in the seal and the light in the merge are the same blue, and the audience should feel that connection without being told.

### Reactive moments
- Holograms scatter and re-settle when Lawyer Zero walks through them
- The gavel strike sends a visible ripple through the energy veins in the floor
- The 0:38 silence: everything ambient slows to half speed. The room holds its breath with the audience
- During the merge: all ambient particles reverse direction and drift *toward* the two Zeros

> **Why this matters more than it sounds.** A static background reads as an ad with a mascot in front of it. A room that breathes, reacts and glows reads as a *world* — and a world is what makes someone forget they're being sold to. This spec is the difference between the two.

## What the animation tests proved

1. **Start-frame + motion-only prompting works.** Feed an approved still, describe *only* what moves, and the design survives. Never re-describe the character or the room in a video prompt.
2. **Decline every style preset.** Higgsfield suggested "IN THE DARK" on the first pass — accepting a stylistic preset re-renders the look and undoes the costume-swap work. Always decline and generate literally.
3. **One camera move per shot.** Compound moves degrade fast.
4. **Generate 5s, cut to 3–4s.** The final second is consistently the weakest.
5. **The same negative list applies to video prompts:** `do not restyle, do not redesign, do not age him, do not make it realistic`.

## Honest production note

These tests are proof of *look*, not proof of *film*. AI video handles a slow push-in on a static hero pose well. It does not reliably handle: Judge Zero descending the bench stairs with a cane, two characters walking toward each other and meeting on a mark, the smile in Shot 17, or lip-sync on dialogue.

**The realistic path:** these approved frames become the model sheets and colour keys. A 3D or 2D animation team builds the film to match them. AI generation stays in the pipeline for boards, style frames, background plates, and ambient inserts — which is exactly what it's good at.
