# EST I Math — Question Archetype Library

**Artifact 2 of 8.** Evidence tiers and the frequency vocabulary are defined in
[`01-est-exam-dna.md` §0](01-est-exam-dna.md). Counts here are over the same
200 classified items (4 forms × 50).

> No exam content. Archetypes are described by their *structure* — what is
> given, what is asked, what makes them hard. Where an illustration helps, it is
> one we wrote, not one from a reference form.

---

## 1. How to read this library, and its central finding

The library has two levels, because the corpus behaves differently at each.

**Family level — nearly fixed.** 18 families. **17 of the 18 appear in all four
forms.** The share each family takes is stable enough to be a generation quota.

**Archetype level — nearly disjoint.** 191 distinct archetypes across 200 items.
Two of the four forms use **50 distinct archetypes in 50 items**; the other two
repeat exactly once each, and both repeats are a stimulus-sharing pair. Across
forms, only **7 of 191** archetypes appear in more than one form.

> **T2 — the design rule.** *Hold the family mix; refresh the archetype.* A form
> is built by drawing the required number of items from each family and then
> instantiating each with an archetype that does not appear elsewhere in the
> form and, wherever possible, did not appear in the previous form either.

This is why the library is organised as families-with-inventories rather than as
a flat list of templates. **A generator that treats the archetype list as a
sampling pool will produce forms with repeats and will read as synthetic.**

## 3. Family inventories

Each entry gives the archetypes observed, with how many forms carried each. An
archetype marked ×2 is one of the seven that crossed forms.

### A13 — Data: read a display · 30 items · 4/4 · core recurring

The family splits into five behaviours, which is why it is so large:

| Behaviour | Items | Description |
|---|---:|---|
| **Interpret a parameter in context** | 5 | Given a model or a curve, say what a constant, a rate or an intercept *means*. Never "solve"; always "what does this represent". |
| **Single read** | 4 | One value or one comparison off a chart. The exam's cheapest items. |
| **Scan under a condition** | 4 | Find every category satisfying a stated threshold; find the first crossing point; find the largest by an aggregate rather than by a single bar. |
| **Read then compute** | 12 | Read one or two values, then percentage / rate / weighted mean / unit conversion on top. The bulk of the family. |
| **Judge a claim** | 5 | Which statement about the display is true; what could explain it; which measure is unchanged. Includes the exam's clearest inference items. |

Observed archetypes: `parameter-interpret` ×2 (one form, one stimulus),
`parameter-interpret-constant`, `parameter-interpret-rate`,
`parameter-interpret-intercept`, `bar-read-single`, `bar-compare-single`,
`bar-multi-condition-scan`, `grouped-bar-aggregate`, `graph-comprehension-axes`,
`scaled-axis-read`, `read-then-percent-scaled`, `graph-read-multi-step-rate`,
`aggregate-over-groups`, `whole-sum-then-percent`, `bar-percent-to-other-category`,
`event-anchored-series-scan`, `percent-decrease-two-series`,
`two-model-intersection-read`, `physics-graph-unit-conversion`,
`pie-percent-to-count-diff`, `pie-ratio-on-remainder`, `two-way-table-denominator`,
`ordinal-row-reference-percent`, `percent-change-direction-base`,
`rate-of-change-two-rows`, `mean-of-subset-ordering-trap`,
`weighted-mean-frequency`, `qualitative-claim-eval`, `causal-inference-no-calc`.

Three archetypes in this family deserve naming because they carry most of its
difficulty and each recurs in spirit if not in letter:

- **Wrong-denominator items.** A two-way table where the stem names a *specific*
  denominator ("out of all employees", "of the students who…"), and all three
  distractors are the same numerator over a different plausible denominator.
  **Core recurring in substance** — every form contains at least one item whose
  entire distractor set is a denominator choice.
- **Scaled-axis items.** Both axes labelled "in hundreds" or "in thousands", so
  the graph reading must be multiplied twice. Observed twice, one form
  (**form-specific** as an explicit device) but the underlying unit-carelessness
  trap appears in all four.
- **Ordinal-reference items.** The stem refers to "the second day", "the last
  five years" — and the table is sorted in the opposite order to the natural
  reading. **Occasional**, 2 forms, and one of the exam's cleanest traps.

### A06 — Quadratics & parabolas · 16 items · 4/4 · core recurring

`parameters-from-roots` ×2, plus `quadratic-range`, `vertex-abscissa`,
`vertex-form-complete-square`, `axis-of-symmetry-parameter`, `vertex-paraphrased`,
`quadratic-through-three-points`, `line-parabola-intersection`,
`curve-line-select-root`, `sum-of-roots`, `product-of-roots`,
`roots-ordered-select`, `absvalue-select-positive-root`,
`parabola-inscribed-square`, `tangent-at-vertex-concept`.

**T2 — Vieta's relations appear in 3 of 4 forms** (`sum-of-roots`,
`product-of-roots`, `cubic-factors-sum-roots`, `biquadratic-product-roots` across
A06/A07). They are **not on the reference sheet**. This is one of the exam's
most reliable recall demands.

**T2 — "which root" is a repeated device.** Four items across three forms give
two roots and ask for a specific one: the positive one, the larger, the smaller
under a stated ordering. The other root is always an option.

**T3 — vertex questions are asked under a paraphrase.** "The abscissa of the
vertex", "where the graph changes direction", "the axis of symmetry" — three
different surface forms for one computation. A generator that always says
"vertex" will be recognisably narrower than the corpus.

### A12 — Percentages & proportional reasoning · 13 items · 4/4 · core recurring

`composed-percentage-chain`, `backwards-fraction-chain`, `reverse-percentage`,
`percent-increase-simple`, `percent-with-wrong-flip`, `percent-change-base`,
`ratio-three-part-largest`, `fixed-overhead-integer-floor`,
`fixed-plus-variable-compare`, `fixed-plus-rate-total`, `budget-integer-floor`,
`conditional-branch-word`, `two-condition-rate-word`.

Two sub-patterns are worth naming:

- **The percentage chain, forwards and backwards.** Forwards: a percentage of a
  percentage of a total, with each intermediate value offered as a distractor.
  Backwards: a fraction takes a share, a percentage of *the remainder* takes
  another, a stated count is what is left — recover the total, then report a
  share of it. Both observed; **occasional** (2 forms) as an explicit pair, and
  the backwards form is markedly the harder of the two.
- **Fixed overhead plus a rate, then floor to an integer.** Observed 3 times
  across 3 forms (**occasional**). The two named errors are always available:
  omit the fixed cost, or fail to floor.

### A05 — Lines in the plane · 12 items · 4/4 · core recurring

`collinear-points` ×2, `slope-nonstandard-form`, `line-two-points-xintercept`,
`perpendicular-through-midpoint`, `perpendicular-NOT-item`,
`perpendicular-parameters-composite`, `parallel-parameters-composite`,
`parallel-slope-standard-form`, `param-from-point-then-slope`,
`table-linear-missing`, `linear-table-two-unknowns`.

**T2 — the recall load is the point.** Slope, midpoint and the distance formula
are all absent from the reference sheet, and this family leans on all three.

**T2 — "two lines carrying parameters" is a repeated shape.** Two forms build an
item where two lines are given with unknown coefficients, a parallel or
perpendicular relation is stated, and the stem asks for a *combination* of the
parameters, never a parameter alone.

### A14 — Statistics: summarise a data set · 12 items · exactly 3 per form · 4/4

`mean-from-table`, `line-graph-mean`, `mean-with-added-element`, `mean-to-target`,
`measure-of-centre-compare`, `stem-and-leaf-median`, `interquartile-range`,
`cumulative-frequency-mode`, `best-fit-slope-read`, `scatter-column-sum-range`,
`scatter-trend-plausibility`, `residual-actual-vs-predicted`.

**T3 — exactly three per form, every form.** The most stable count in the corpus.

**T2 — the statistical vocabulary runs well past the digital SAT's.** The corpus
asks for interquartile range, the median off a stem-and-leaf plot, the mode from
a cumulative-frequency polygon, and a **residual** — actual minus model-predicted
— from a scatterplot with the regression equation supplied. A generated form
that stops at mean/median/mode is under-specifying the family.

**T2 — the mean is asked in four different directions**: compute it; compute it
after adding a member; find the value needed to reach a target mean; and compare
two data sets' measures of centre. All four appear.

### A01 — Linear equations & solving · 11 items · 4/4 · core recurring

`linear-solve` ×2, `fraction-equation-solve`, `multi-step-linear-distribute`,
`chained-substitution`, `linear-verbal-substitution`, `linear-composite`,
`scale-equation-composite`, `translate-composite-target`, `no-solution-parameter`,
`literal-system`.

**T2 — the plain "solve for x" item is rare.** Only two of eleven ask for x
directly. The rest wrap the solve in something: a value stated in words rather
than digits, a target that is a combination of x, a parameter that must make the
equation have no solution, or coefficients that are letters.

### A04 — Inequalities · 11 items · 4/4 · core recurring

`compound-ineq-composite-integer` ×2, `inequality-from-graph`,
`inequality-translate-test`, `compound-inequality-point-test`,
`inequality-intersection-unique`, `inequality-smallest-integer`,
`absvalue-inequality-NOT-item`, `inequality-system-quadrant`,
`quadratic-inequality-numberline`, `absvalue-interval-max`.

**T2 — strict-versus-inclusive is the family's defining trap.** Seven items
across all four forms hinge on whether an endpoint is included, and the excluded
endpoint is an option every time.

**T2 — the "smallest / largest integer such that" shape recurs in three forms**,
twice in near-identical structure: solve a compound inequality, shift by a
constant, then take the extreme integer. Of all the archetypes in the corpus this
is the closest thing to a reusable template.

### A09 — Functions: composition & evaluation · 11 items · 4/4 · core recurring

`function-eval`, `composition-evaluate`, `symbolic-composition`,
`composite-eval-difference`, `function-of-function-eval`, `table-from-function-value`,
`multi-valued-can-be`, `multi-curve-graph-sum`, `which-student-multi-claim`,
`graph-roman-numeral`, `absvalue-graph-identify`.

**T2 — composition is asked both numerically and symbolically**, and the
numeric version's distractor set is a full grid: the value of the inner function
alone, the value of the outer alone, and the composition applied in the reverse
order.

**T2 — multi-claim items live here.** Two shapes: four named students each
asserting one property of a curve, "which student could be right"; and three
Roman-numeral statements with subset options. Both are among the exam's clearest
Reasoning items.

### A10 — Exponents, radicals & complex numbers · 11 items · 4/4 · core recurring

`exponent-laws-simplify`, `exponent-law-substitution`, `exponent-radical-equation`,
`exponent-common-base`, `exponent-equation-composite`, `radical-equation-parameter`,
`radical-simplify-cancels-zero`, `radical-index-simplify`, `radicals-sign-constrained`,
`substitute-into-radical`, `complex-number-composite`.

**T2 — the exponent and radical laws are not on the reference sheet** and this
family exists to test them.

**T3 — complex numbers appear in 2 of 4 forms** (`i` as a root of a biquadratic;
a complex quotient). **Occasional.** They are legitimate EST content and should
appear in roughly half of generated forms — not every one.

**Named sub-pattern: the expression that collapses.** One item's three radical
terms cancel exactly, and the correct option is `0`. Form-specific, but a real
device worth keeping in the repertoire.

### A07 — Polynomials & factoring · 10 items · 4/4 · core recurring

`targeted-coefficient-extract` ×2, `factor-polynomial`,
`coefficient-matching-identity`, `cubic-factors-sum-roots`,
`biquadratic-product-roots`, `cubic-real-solution-count`, `remainder-theorem`,
`remainder-theorem-composite`, `divisibility-remainder-parameter`.

**T2 — the remainder theorem appears in 3 of 4 forms**: plain, with the answer
scaled, and as "find the parameter that makes it divisible". **Core recurring in
substance.**

**T2 — "the coefficient of x^n in a product" appears in 2 forms** and is one of
the exam's few genuine time traps: full expansion is slow, only two term-pairs
contribute, and the distractors are "found one pair and stopped".

### A08 — Rational expressions & functions · 9 items · 4/4 · core recurring

`rational-expression-simplify`, `rational-undefined`, `range-of-rational`,
`asymptote-count`, `complex-fraction-simplify`, `rational-equation-composite`,
`inverse-function-rational`, `inverse-value-evaluate`, `inverse-vs-reciprocal`.

**T2 — domain and range are asked as distinct things and confused deliberately.**
An item asking for the *range* of a rational function offers the *domain*
exclusion as a distractor. An item asking for an inverse offers the reciprocal.

**T3 — asymptote counting appears once.** Rare, but it is squarely inside this
exam's syllabus in a way it is not inside the SAT's.

### A15 — Probability & counting · 9 items · 4/4 · core recurring

`probability-number-property`, `prob-inclusion-exclusion-half`,
`prob-replacement-order-grid`, `prob-complement-no-replacement`,
`probability-squared-target`, `expected-value`, `counting-perm-vs-comb`,
`symbolic-counting-formula`, `constrained-enumeration`.

**T2 — replacement and ordering are the family's two axes**, and the corpus
builds full 2×2 distractor grids over them: with/without replacement crossed
with ordered/unordered. Two forms do this explicitly.

**T2 — the stem modifies the probability.** Three of nine ask not for P but for
half of P, the square of P, or P of a complement. The unmodified value is always
an option. (See artifact 4, class D1.)

**T3 — counting is asked symbolically as well as numerically.** One item asks for
a formula in terms of *k* rather than a number.

### A16 — Geometry: angles & triangles · 9 items · 4/4 · core recurring

`angle-chase-parallel`, `angle-chase-algebraic`, `angle-chase-two-variables`,
`always-true-parallel-angles`, `similar-triangles-parallel-composite`,
`similar-right-triangles-altitudes`, `special-triangle-45`,
`trig-ratio-composite-figure`, `trig-costumed-algebra`.

**T2 — "Figure not drawn to scale" is printed on 8 items across all four forms.**
It is an explicit device, not a legal disclaimer: it removes measuring and
estimating from the diagram as a strategy.

**T2 — trigonometric ratios are not on the reference sheet** and two forms
require them.

**Named sub-pattern: trig-costumed algebra.** An item that displays sine and
cosine but requires no trigonometry at all — a linear rearrangement of the given
relation produces the requested combination directly. Form-specific, and a
genuinely elegant device.

### A17 — Geometry: circles, area & solids · 9 items · 4/4 · core recurring

`circle-inside-boundary`, `circle-circumference-from-grid`,
`circle-centre-point-diameter`, `circle-roman-numeral`, `cone-slant-vs-height`,
`prism-volume-ratio-dimension`, `square-from-diagonal`,
`coordinate-rectangle-rotated`, `parallelogram-centre-midpoint`.

**T2 — coordinate geometry is where this family mostly lives.** Five of nine put
the figure on axes and require the distance or midpoint formula, neither of which
is given.

**T2 — the "wrong but adjacent measure" distractor is this family's signature**:
radius offered when the diameter is asked; slant height substituted for
perpendicular height; the diagonal squared offered as the area.

### A03 — Systems of equations · 7 items · 4/4 · core recurring

`two-purchase-system-third-bundle`, `two-price-system-money`,
`system-composite-target`, `system-composite-product`, `system-one-coordinate`,
`infinite-solutions-identify`, `parametric-system-symbolic`.

**T2 — five of seven never ask for x or y.** They ask for a linear combination,
a product, one named coordinate, or the money produced by one of the two
categories. This family is the corpus's densest concentration of derived targets.

**T3 — the "two purchases, price a third basket" word problem** is the family's
most recognisable shape.

### A11 — Growth & variation · 7 items · 4/4 · core recurring

`exponential-growth-offbyone`, `compound-interest-forward`,
`compound-interest-reverse`, `direct-variation-square`,
`direct-variation-transformed`, `inverse-variation-composite`,
`linear-extrapolation-daycount`.

**T2 — direct or inverse variation appears in 3 of 4 forms**, always with a twist:
the variable is squared, cubed, or itself a multiple.

**T2 — period counting is the family's trap.** Every growth item in the corpus
turns on getting the number of periods right, and every one offers the answer
with one period too many or too few.

**T3 — compound interest is asked in both directions**, including solving
backwards for the present value needed to reach a target. That is beyond typical
SAT scope.

### A18 — Number properties & logic · 6 items · 4/4 · core recurring

`roman-numeral-properties`, `roman-numeral-sign-reasoning`, `vertical-line-roman`,
`consecutive-integers-sum`, `absolute-value-nested-count`,
`sum-product-then-half-greater`.

**T2 — every form contains at least one Roman-numeral multi-statement item**,
across this family and A09/A17: five items, 4 of 4 forms. The options are always
subsets ("I only", "II only", "I and III only", "I, II and III") and the design
requires evaluating each statement independently.

### A02 — Modelling: build the expression · 7 items · **3/4** · occasional

`model-build-equation` ×3, `proportion-rearrange` ×2, `model-build-absvalue`,
`literal-formula-rearrange`.

**T2 — the tested skill is translation, not solving.** The stem asks which
*equation* or *expression* models the situation; the distractors are structurally
wrong models — an operation reversed, a factor not distributed, a term not
doubled, a unit conversion applied to the wrong term. **Never an arithmetic
slip**, because there is no arithmetic.

**T2 — rearranging a given formula for a named variable** is the family's other
half, and one instance wraps it in a paragraph of science exposition that the
mathematics does not need. The reading load is the difficulty.

### The derived-target device — the corpus's most important modifier

Counted separately because it cuts across everything: **the stem asks for
something other than the quantity the work naturally produces.**

| Form | Items with a derived target | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Form 1 | 4 | 8% |
| Form 2 | 10 | 20% |
| Form 3 | 12 | 24% |
| Form 5 | 8 | 16% |
| **Pooled** | **34** | **17%** |

**T2 — core recurring, present in all four forms, at 8–24%.** The range is wide
enough that a generation rule must be a range, not a point: **6–12 items per
form**, which spans the observed 4–12 while excluding Form 1's unusually low count
as a floor.

Shapes observed: ask for `k+4` when the work gives `k`; for `−5tg` when it gives
`tg`; for the diameter when it gives the radius; for the sum of three integers
when it gives the first; for half of the greater root; for the square of a
probability; for twice a remainder; for the length rather than the width; for the
money from one ticket class rather than the count.

**T2 — and the un-derived value is offered as an option nearly every time.**
That is the mechanism, not a coincidence. Artifact 4 treats it as distractor
class D1.

## 5. What a generator must take from this artifact

1. **Draw by family, to the quotas in §2**, then instantiate.
2. **Never repeat an archetype inside a form.** Two of four reference forms
   achieve 50 distinct archetypes in 50 items; that is the bar.
3. **Refresh archetypes between forms.** Only 7 of 191 crossed forms in the
   corpus; a generated series should aim for a comparable turnover.
4. **A13 gets 6–10 items** and must span all five of its behaviours, not just
   "read a value".
5. **A14 gets exactly 3**, spanning the wider statistical vocabulary.
6. **Geometry (A16 + A17) gets 4–6 items total** — the published band from
   artifact 1, and the constraint most easily violated.
7. **6–12 items carry a derived target**, each with the un-derived value offered.
8. **Attach the core-recurring devices deliberately**: a shared stimulus on 8–12
   items, "not drawn to scale" on 1–4, a boundary discrimination on 1–2, exactly
   one Roman-numeral item.
9. **Rotate the rare devices** rather than using them every time: complex numbers,
   NOT-items, stem modifiers, "none of the above", four-computation options and
   object-valued option sets belong in roughly half of forms each.
