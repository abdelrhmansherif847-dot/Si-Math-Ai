# EST I Math — Authenticity Model

**Artifact 5 of 8.** Evidence tiers: [`01-est-exam-dna.md` §0](01-est-exam-dna.md).

> What makes an item read as **EST**, rather than as SAT or ACT wearing an EST
> hat. Artifacts 2–4 govern what an item *tests*; this one governs how it
> *sounds and looks*. A form can satisfy every structural constraint and still be
> obviously synthetic, and the tells are almost all here.

---

## 1. The single most distinctive feature: French-influenced mathematical register

**T2 — this is the strongest authenticity signal in the corpus, present in all
four forms, and the one most likely to be missed.**

EST I Math is written in the mathematical English of a region whose secondary
mathematics tradition is French-influenced. The vocabulary is not American
test-English:

| The corpus says | An American test would say |
|---|---|
| "the **abscissa** of the vertex" | "the x-coordinate of the vertex" |
| "the **ordinate** *b* is equal to" | "the y-value is" |
| "the curve **admits** a center of symmetry" | "the graph has a center of symmetry" |
| "How many asymptotes does the curve of the function *f* **admit**?" | "How many asymptotes does the graph have?" |
| "the function *f* **defined by** *f*(x) = …" | "the function *f*(x) = …" |
| "Line *m* is parallel to the line with equation …" | "Line *m* is parallel to *y* = …" |
| "the **straight line** *d* of equation …" | "the line …" |
| "*d* and *d′* are two parallel lines" | "lines *ℓ*₁ and *ℓ*₂ are parallel" |

**T2 — lines are named `d`, `d′`, `d₁`, `d₂`, `m`.** Not `ℓ`, not `line 1`. `d` is
from *droite*. Circles are named `C`, `C₁`, `C₂`.

**T2 — the answer stem often trails into the options rather than asking a
question.** "The median of the data is:", "The value of *x*₂ is:", "The inverse
function of *y* = 400*x* is:". Roughly a third of items use this colon form
rather than a question mark. An exam written entirely in "What is…?" is
recognisably not this one.

**T3 — the English is competent but not native, and consistently so.** "Solving
the equation above yields to *y* =", "Assume being given three consecutive even
integers", "what percentage of the students answered…", "How much money would you
need to deposit today…". We are **not** recommending that generated items imitate
grammatical errors — that would be both disrespectful and unhelpful to students.
The point is the *register*: formal, slightly bookish, translated-feeling
mathematical English. Write in that register; write it correctly.

## 2. People, places and contexts

**T2 — given names are predominantly Arabic and Levantine**, with a minority of
Anglophone names mixed in without comment. Observed across the corpus: Amina,
Amira, Amir, Ahmad, Bilal, Faten, Lara, Maya, Mirna, Mona, Nassim, Ramy, Rima,
Samir, Tarek — alongside Adam, Alice, Bob, Troy, Tom, and one "Mrs. Jana".

**T2 — surnames are essentially never used.** One "Mrs. Jana" (a teacher, and
that is a title plus a given name) in 200 items. People are first names only.

**T2 — the geography is regional or anonymised.** Cairo and Egypt appear by name;
otherwise "city *A*", "city *B*", "country *X*", "village *A*", "village *B*", "a
high school", "an electronics store". There are no American place names, no state
abbreviations, no US-specific institutions.

**T2 — contexts are ordinary commerce, school and civic life.** Observed:
salaries and sales commission; a flower shop; a bookstore; taxi tariffs; a
printing house; a magazine subscription; brochure printing; gold prices;
electricity use by household appliance; laptops repaired by an engineer;
new-car sales by brand; phone brands among school staff; a school prom vote;
survey responses on pandemic concerns; COVID-19 case counts; population growth;
production of highlighters and of bottled water; a basketball player's scoring;
push-ups; students' exam scores and absences.

**T3 — brands appear by name.** Huawei, Samsung, iPhone, Hyundai, Honda. This is
unusual for a US standardised test and is a genuine EST texture. Use sparingly
and neutrally — as category labels, never as endorsement, and never with any
claim about a brand's quality.

**T2 — real data is cited when used.** One item names *Worldometer* as its source
for pandemic figures. Where a generated item uses real-world data, name the
source in the stem; where it uses invented data, say nothing (the corpus never
pretends).

**Constraint we impose (not observed, deliberate).** Pandemic case counts appear
in the corpus. For a live Egyptian exam-prep product used by teenagers, we will
**not** generate items about disease case counts, mortality, conflict, or any
other distressing subject even though the reference forms do. This is a product
decision recorded as a departure, not a finding.

## 3. Units, currency and numbers

**T2 — currency is US dollars (`$`), even in Egyptian contexts.** No Egyptian
pounds anywhere in 200 items. Amounts are written `$1,300`, `$0.3`, `$19,750`.

**T2 — length, distance and mass are metric.** km, cm, m, km/h.

**T2 — but volume is imperial.** Gallons appear twice — fuel consumption in
kilometres per gallon, and water production in gallons. The corpus mixes metric
distance with imperial volume without apology, in the same paper.

**T2 — units are repeated in every option, not factored into the stem.** "A. 5
*cm*  B. 8 *cm*  C. 10 *cm*  D. 12 *cm*", and the unit is set in italic. Likewise
"22.5 unit squared", "$16.6", "1,678". A generated form that writes "…in cm?" and
then gives bare numbers is out of register.

**T2 — "unit squared", not "square units".** The corpus writes area answers as
"22.5 unit squared".

**T2 — thousands carry commas** (`1,500`, `10,000`, `94,640`) and decimals are
written to the precision the answer needs, uniformly across an option set
(`0.456 / 0.479 / 0.501 / 0.527`; `1,137.2 / 1,351.82 / 1,368.99 / 1,622.18`).

**T3 — numbers are chosen to be calculator-tractable but not artificially
round.** Gold prices to two decimals, a salary of `$10.7` per hour, `2.5%`
interest, `44 cents` per brochure. The corpus does not sanitise its numbers into
tidy integers, which is the commonest way a generated item betrays itself.

## 4. Stem conventions

**T2 — shared-stimulus headings are bold and take one of three fixed forms:**

- `Questions 20 to 22 refer to the information below.`
- `Questions 24 and 25 refer to the following information below.`
- `Questions 7 and 8 refer to the following graph.`

Two questions take "*X* and *Y*"; three or more take "*X* to *Y*". The heading
always precedes the stimulus, and the descriptive prose may sit either above or
below the graphic.

**T2 — `(Figure not drawn to scale)` is set in italic parentheses**, and is
placed either immediately after the stem sentence or on its own line before the
options. Eight items, all four forms.

**T2 — figures are referred to as "the figure above", "the adjacent figure", "the
figure below", "the graph above", "the adjacent table".** "Adjacent" is used when
the graphic sits beside the text rather than above it — a layout-aware phrase an
American test would not use.

**T2 — display equations sit centred above their stem**, unnumbered, with the
stem below beginning "In the system of equations above…", "From the system of
equations above…", "The equation above has no real solutions…". Roughly one item
in ten uses this layout.

**T2 — Roman-numeral items list `I.` `II.` `III.` under the stem**, and the four
options are always subsets in ascending size: "I only", "II only", "I and II
only", "I, II, and III". The corpus uses the serial comma.

**T2 — options are labelled `A.` `B.` `C.` `D.` with a full stop.**

**T3 — the stem states rounding when it matters**, in the corpus's own phrasing:
"to the nearest *cm*", "rounded to the nearest tenth", "to the nearest whole
number", "approximately". "Approximately" is used both in the stem and inside
options ("It decreased by 27.17%").

## 5. Figure and chart conventions

**T2 — the chart vocabulary is wide and the styling is workmanlike, not
polished.** Observed: 3-D exploded pie charts with the percentage inside each
slice and the category label in a callout box; grouped bar charts with a boxed
legend; line graphs with the value printed beside each vertex; scatterplots with
a dashed line of best fit and two of its points labelled; two-series line graphs
distinguished by solid versus dashed; a stem-and-leaf plot with an explicit
`Key: 3 | 6 means 36 minutes`; a decreasing cumulative-frequency polygon; three
labelled curves on one axis set.

**T2 — axes are labelled with the quantity and, where scaled, the scale.**
"Salary (in thousands dollars)", "Number of appliances, in hundreds", "Cost, in
hundreds of dollars", "Highlighters, in hundreds of units". The scale in the axis
label rather than in the tick values is itself a difficulty device (artifact 3).

**T2 — geometric figures are line drawings with labelled vertices in italic
capitals**, right angles marked with a small square, and lengths written beside
the segment without a unit when the stem supplies it. Parallel lines are drawn as
long horizontals with a transversal; circles are drawn with a marked centre.

**T2 — tables have a shaded header row** and are centred. Two-way tables put the
categories across the top and the groups down the side.

**T3 — nothing in the corpus is drawn to a designed grid system.** The figures
look like a competent textbook's, not like a design system's. Our own
`figure-system.css` grammar will make generated forms *cleaner* than the
reference forms. That is an acceptable and deliberate divergence — a student is
not helped by imitating a scan artefact — but it means figure fidelity cannot be
judged by visual comparison against the corpus.

## 6. What an EST item is *not*

Stated as contrasts, because the fastest way to write an inauthentic EST item is
to write a good SAT or ACT one.

**Not the digital SAT.** No modules, no adaptive second stage, no student-produced
responses, no reference sheet containing the quadratic formula or slope. The SAT
supplies more formulas and asks for fewer of them from memory. The SAT's
statistics stops well short of interquartile range from a stem-and-leaf plot,
mode from a cumulative-frequency polygon, or a residual against a supplied cubic
model — all of which the EST corpus asks. And the SAT never uses "abscissa".

**Not the ACT.** The ACT ramps steadily across 60 items in 60 minutes; the EST is
50 items in 75 minutes with an easy on-ramp and then no ramp at all. The ACT uses
five options with alternating letter sets; the EST uses four, A–D, always. The
ACT's register is plain American English; the EST's is formal and
French-influenced.

**Not a textbook exercise.** Every item has a stated context or a stated
mathematical object; there are no bare "simplify" drills without a question
around them, and every wrong option is a named student error rather than a
neighbouring number.

## 7. The authenticity checklist

Applied per item by artifact 7, and per form by artifact 8.

**Register**
1. Mathematical vocabulary drawn from the corpus's own register where a choice
   exists — abscissa/ordinate, "admits", "defined by", lines named `d`/`m`.
2. Roughly a third of stems in the colon form rather than as a question.
3. Formal, unhurried phrasing. Correct English, not native-American-test English.

**People and context**
4. Given names only, weighted to Arabic and Levantine names, some Anglophone.
5. Regional or anonymised geography. No US place names or institutions.
6. Ordinary commerce, school and civic contexts.
7. **No distressing subject matter** (our constraint, §2).

**Numbers and units**
8. US dollars; metric length; imperial volume permitted.
9. Units repeated in every option, italicised; "unit squared" for area.
10. Commas in thousands; uniform decimal precision across an option set.
11. Numbers calculator-tractable but not artificially round.

**Layout**
12. `Questions X and Y` / `Questions X to Y refer to…` headings, bold.
13. `(Figure not drawn to scale)` in italic parentheses where the figure must not
    be measured.
14. Display equations centred above the stem, referenced as "above".
15. Options `A.`–`D.`; Roman numerals `I.`–`III.` with subset options in
    ascending size and the serial comma.
16. Axis labels carry the quantity and any scale factor.

**T4 note — one thing we cannot check.** We have no EST item written by the
publisher outside a preparation guide. If the live exam's register differs from
its own practice material, this artifact inherits that difference. Nothing in the
corpus suggests it does, but the possibility is recorded rather than ignored.
